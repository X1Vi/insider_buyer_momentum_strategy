--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART A — COPY-PASTE PROMPT FOR CHATGPT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You are a quantitative strategist. I run a NSE (India) breakout-momentum strategy
and want your independent, HONEST analysis. Do NOT flatter the strategy — actively
look for why it might fail.

## Strategy definition
- Universe: NSE CASH large-cap stocks and ETFs (e.g., SBIN, JSWSTEEL, MAFANG, BANKBEES).
- Signal (daily bars): a "high-volume upside breakout" = on day t,
    close[t] > the highest close/high of the prior 20 days  AND
    volume[t] >= 3x the average volume of the prior 20 days.
- Thesis: after such a breakout, a stock in a confirmed uptrend tends to make
  further higher highs (momentum continuation).
- Trend filter: only take signals with positive 1-year (252-trading-day) return and
  price above its 50- and 200-day moving averages. NO signals on falling stocks.
- Entry: buy the breakout close, or a pullback retest of the broken 20-day high.
- Stop: below the breakout level (the 20-day high now support).
- Exit: trailing stop behind each new higher high; exit if price closes back below the
  20-day high; exit if no new high within 20 days.

## Data I can give you
Daily OHLCV (open, high, low, close, volume) for each symbol, 2023-2026.
Columns: ts,open,high,low,close,volume.

## What I need from you
1. Backtest this signal on the data with STRICT no-look-ahead: signal on day t uses
   only data <= t; outcomes measured on t+1..t+20. Deduplicate consecutive signals
   (gap >= 10 days between trades). Report per symbol AND pooled: number of trades,
   hit-rate (share that reached a higher high), mean #higher highs, average forward
   20-day return, win rate, and equity curve.
2. SPLIT the sample in half (train/test by time) and report both. If the effect
   disappears out-of-sample, say so plainly.
3. Compare signal days against a CONTROL: all non-signal days. If the breakout adds
   no edge over random days, say so.
4. BIAS CHECK — explicitly flag each:
   - survivorship bias (my universe is today's large caps; delisted names are absent),
   - small sample sizes per name,
   - in-sample strategy development on the same data,
   - costs: assume ~0.1% per side; does the edge survive fees?
   - market regime: 2023-2026 was mostly a bull market — how would this behave in a bear/choppy market?
5. RANK the symbols by highest odds of continuing to higher highs, using momentum,
   downside resilience, forward return, and penalizing tiny samples. Give a clear
   #1 pick with reasoning.
6. For the #1 pick give concrete: entry trigger level, entry zone, stop level, and
   exit rule, expressed as chart levels an investor can draw on the daily chart.
7. Tell me the biggest reason this strategy is likely to underperform, and one
   concrete improvement.

Rules: be rigorous, quantify everything, and explicitly state uncertainty. If a
conclusion is weak or the data insufficient, say "not enough evidence" rather than guess.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART B — WHEN TO TAKE ENTRY AND EXIT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ENTRY — only when the setup is confirmed:
1. Uptrend confirmed: 1-year momentum > 0, price above 50 & 200 SMA.
   (Skip breakouts on falling stocks — they are the low-odds trap.)
2. Breakout trigger: live price breaks ABOVE the 20-day high (the screener's
   TRADEABLE TODAY / lBRK flag).
3. Optional confirmation: volume >= 3x the 20-day average (adds confidence,
   but the breakout itself is the edge).

How to enter (pick one):
- Breakout-day close: simplest — buy the confirmation, but you buy near the high
  so risk is wider.
- Pullback retest (better reward/risk): wait for price to pull back to the broken
  20-day high (now support) and enter there.

STOP (set before entry):
- Below the breakout level (the 20-day high, now support) or below the breakout
  bar's low — whichever is tighter.
- Keep risk small: ~1-2% of the account per trade. If the stop distance is >~5%,
  the trade is too late/too wide — skip it.

EXIT — the trade works while it keeps making higher highs:
- Trailing stop: ratchet the stop up behind each new higher high (e.g. trail at
  the 10-day low, or ~2-3x ATR below the latest swing high). Exit when price
  breaks that trailing support (the higher-high streak ends).
- Breakout-failure exit: if price closes back BELOW the 20-day high, exit.
- Time exit: if no meaningful new high within ~20 days, the signal failed — exit.
- Optional target: take partial profit on a big volume blow-off, let the rest ride.

HONEST CAVEATS (repeat these to yourself):
- The edge is modest BEFORE costs; fees/slippage can eat it.
- Works in trending markets, fails in chop/bears.
- Per-name sample sizes are tiny; a "100% hit rate" on 5 trades is luck, not truth.
- Survivorship bias inflates results (current large caps only).
- Paper-trade first. Size small. No single backtest number justifies risk.
